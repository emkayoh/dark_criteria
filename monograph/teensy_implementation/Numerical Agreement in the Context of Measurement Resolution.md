### Numerical agreement in the context of measurement resolution

The directed real-time VARPRO implementation and the corresponding full global search typically agreed to approximately five decimal places or better across the fitted parameters. This degree of numerical agreement should be interpreted in the context of the resolution of the underlying measurements. Response time is recorded to the nearest millisecond, corresponding to approximately \(1.7\times10^{-5}\) min. Stimulus intensity is represented by a 16-bit PWM value spanning approximately 48.16 dB, \(10\log_{10}(2^{16}-1)\), giving a nominal quantisation of approximately \(7.35\times10^{-4}\) dB per PWM step. Parameter estimates derived from multiple observations can, of course, be reported at a finer numerical precision than that of an individual observation; measurement quantisation therefore does not impose a strict decimal-place limit on the fitted parameters. Nevertheless, discrepancies between optimisation methods at scales substantially below the resolution of the acquired data are unlikely to have experimental significance. In this context, agreement to five decimal places provides a stringent practical demonstration that the directed real-time refinement and the full global VARPRO search are converging to effectively equivalent solutions, even where their numerical paths and computational costs differ substantially.
